# Community Cold Start: 0 → 1 Checklist

How to take a Web3 gaming community from an empty group to a self-sustaining one. This is a method checklist, not a growth hack list — every item is something you set up *before* you need it, because cold start fails in the preparation, not in the launch post.

---

## 1. Before the group exists （建群前准备）

- [ ] **One-sentence positioning**: who this community is for, and what a member gets here that they can't get elsewhere. If you can't write it in one sentence, the group has no reason to exist yet.
- [ ] **Pick one home platform** (Telegram, Discord, or WeChat) and commit to it. Splitting a cold community across three platforms means three empty rooms instead of one alive one.
- [ ] **Set up the skeleton before inviting anyone**: group name and avatar, a pinned welcome message, channel/topic structure (announcements, general chat, help/support, content/showcase), and basic anti-spam settings (join verification, link permissions, slow mode if needed).
- [ ] **Write the rules down**: what gets removed, what gets a warning, who decides. Short and enforceable beats long and ignored.
- [ ] **Prepare a FAQ**: the ten questions every newcomer asks (how to start, where to download, how rewards/claims work, where to get help). Pin it. Every unanswered repeat question is friction that pushes a newcomer out.
- [ ] **Seed the content shelf**: 5–10 posts ready to publish across the first week (guides, explainers, common mistakes, a roadmap teaser) so the group never looks empty when someone new lands.
- [ ] **Decide who answers**: cold start needs a named human (you, or a moderator) visibly present. Response time in week one is the product.

## 2. Seed users （种子用户来源）

- [ ] **Start with people you already have a relationship with**: past players, existing followers, partner communities. Ten people who know you beat a hundred strangers from an ad.
- [ ] **Borrow audiences honestly**: cross-promotion with adjacent projects/communities, guest posts, AMAs. Offer value to the partner, not just a link drop.
- [ ] **Recruit where the players already talk**: game forums, X/Twitter threads, Reddit/Discord servers of similar games, content creators' comment sections. Contribute first; invite second.
- [ ] **Make joining worth something concrete**: early-member role, early access to a guide or tool, a founding-member event. "Join our community" is not an offer.
- [ ] **Invite in waves, not a flood**: a small wave you can personally greet and onboard beats a spike of strangers who arrive to silence and leave.
- [ ] **Personally welcome the first members**: a real message from a real person in the first minutes sets the culture more than any rules post.

## 3. First-week activity design （首周活动设计）

- [ ] **One anchor event in week one**: an AMA, a live walkthrough, a small contest with a clear end time — something that gives people a reason to show up at the same time.
- [ ] **Daily lightweight prompts**: a question, a poll, a "show your progress" thread. The goal is first posts — a member who has posted once is far more likely to stay.
- [ ] **Lower the bar to participate**: reactions and polls count. Not everyone will write essays; give them cheap ways to be present.
- [ ] **Publish on a visible rhythm**: if you say updates come every day at a set time, keep it. Predictability builds the checking habit.
- [ ] **Showcase members, not just the project**: repost a member's achievement, answer a member's question publicly, credit contributors by name.
- [ ] **Close the week with a recap**: what happened, who joined, what's next week. A recap turns a chat group into a story people are part of.

## 4. Daily operating cadence （日常运营节奏）

- [ ] **Morning pass**: read everything since yesterday, answer unanswered questions, remove spam, note recurring questions for the FAQ.
- [ ] **One content beat per day minimum**: a post, a prompt, a data point, a member spotlight. Silence is the only unforgivable schedule.
- [ ] **Feedback loop, visibly closed**: collect suggestions and bug reports in one place, and post what changed because of them. People repeat feedback when they see it land.
- [ ] **Moderation with a light hand and a fast one**: remove scams and abuse immediately; let off-topic chatter live within reason — over-moderation kills the room faster than noise.
- [ ] **Weekly anchor events**: a fixed slot (weekly AMA, leaderboard post, community game night) that members can anticipate without being reminded.
- [ ] **Escalation path**: know in advance who handles a scam report, a payment dispute, a harassment case — and how fast. Decide before it happens, not during.

## 5. Review metrics （复盘指标）

Track weekly, in a simple table. Vanity numbers (raw member count) go last, not first.

- [ ] **New members vs. members who left** — net growth, and whether joins are accelerating or stalling
- [ ] **First-post rate** — share of new members who post anything within their first week; the single best cold-start health signal
- [ ] **Daily/weekly active speakers** — how many *distinct* people talk, not total message count (five loud members is not an active community)
- [ ] **Question response time** — median time from a member's question to a useful answer
- [ ] **Event participation** — attendees/entries per anchor event, trend over weeks
- [ ] **Repeat-question count** — questions the FAQ should have answered; a rising count means the onboarding material is failing
- [ ] **Conversion to the next step** — members who actually did the thing the community exists for (played, registered, traded, claimed), where measurable

**Review rule**: every week, pick the single worst metric and fix one thing about it next week. Cold start is a sequence of small repairs, not one big launch.
